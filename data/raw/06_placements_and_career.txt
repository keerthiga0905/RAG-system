1. PLACEMENT CELL OVERVIEW AND RESPONSIBILITIES
============================================================

Source: https://cit.edu.in/placements/placement-cell

The Institute has a Placement Cell headed by a staff member holding the rank of a Professor. The Cell is actively involved in securing placements for students graduating from the Institute.

The office maintains close associations with industrial establishments. These establishments conduct pre-placement presentations and campus interviews, and select student representatives from each department to coordinate placement activities.

The Placement Cell provides facilities to conduct group discussions, tests, and interviews. It also has a library and a communication lab to enhance students' general aptitude and communication skills.

The official page states that national and multinational companies visit the Institute every year for campus recruitment, and that several reputed companies seek to be the “Day One” recruiter for each academic year.

Information regarding campus recruitment is regularly displayed to students to help them plan their careers and understand the industries approaching the Institute. At the beginning of the academic year, a general body meeting is conducted for final-year students interested in placement activities. Monthly review meetings are convened to plan future activities.

The Cell coordinates pre-placement activities for senior and junior students and motivates students to pursue higher studies by supporting preparation for competitive examinations such as GATE, TOEFL, GRE, CAT, and GMAT.

The Cell also seeks to promote self-employment by facilitating government-supported financial assistance through the banking sector. Workshops are organized to develop students' entrepreneurial potential.

3. INTERNSHIP INFORMATION
============================================================

A. Official internship programme page
Source: https://cit.edu.in/placements/internship

The page describes internships as structured industry exposure intended to help students apply theoretical knowledge in professional environments. It states that internships form part of the curriculum across undergraduate, postgraduate, and integrated programmes, in alignment with AICTE guidelines and outcome-based education practices.

Objectives listed:
- Bridge academic learning and industrial practice.
- Expose students to engineering, managerial, and research environments.
- Enhance technical competency, problem-solving, and professional ethics.
- Develop communication, teamwork, and time-management skills.
- Strengthen industry–institute interaction and collaborative learning.

Policy framework features listed:
- Mandatory internship requirement for all eligible programmes.
- Academic credits as prescribed in the curriculum.
- Duration defined by programme structure and semester requirements.
- Faculty mentorship.
- Assessment and reporting through internship reports, reviews, and presentations.

Internship types listed:
- Industry internships in manufacturing, IT, consulting, design, and service sectors.
- Research internships with R&D organizations, laboratories, and academic research centres.
- Start-up / entrepreneurial internships.
- Government / public-sector internships.

Duration may include short-term internships during semester breaks, semester-long internships, and extended internships for integrated and postgraduate programmes. The page does not give a single duration applicable to every programme.

Assessment methods listed: host-organization completion certificate, student internship report, presentation/viva voce according to departmental norms, and continuous assessment by faculty mentors.

B. Placement Report, placement year 2021–2022
Source: https://cit.edu.in/uploads/epdf_documents/d5c3b7e01e8135345c7fcd604060cd8c.pdf

The report says M.Sc. (Software Systems, Data Science, Decision and Computing Sciences) and MCA programmes mandate a six-month internship programme during their 10th and 6th semesters respectively. It states that the majority of these internships are converted into job offers at the end of the internship programme. It also says some companies offer internships to UG students pursuing fast-track programmes and that PG students are provided six-month internships by leading firms where they can pursue project work.

Note: The general internship page describes duration as programme-dependent; the 2021–2022 report gives specific six-month information for the named programmes. Preserve these as statements from different source contexts rather than treating either as a universal rule for every current programme.

10. CONFLICTS, LIMITATIONS, AND MISSING INFORMATION
============================================================

1. Placement-year label conflict in the 50-page Placement Report:
The report's introduction says it covers placement year 2021–2022. However, multiple tables and the narrative on later pages explicitly say “Placement Statistics 2022–2023” and “Overview of Placement 2022–2023.” Both labels are preserved in this file.

2. Conflicting placed-student counts for 2022–2023 within the same PDF:
- The overall-data table labelled “Placement Statistics 2022–2023” reports 757 placed students.
- The six-year historical table reports 782 placed students for 2022–2023.
- The narrative overview also says 757 students secured a job, with 560 UG, 41 PG, and 156 Computing.
These figures are not reconciled in the source. Do not merge or select one without further official clarification.

3. Different figures may refer to different table definitions:
The report has programme-wise, overall-data, category-level, and historical summary tables. Denominators and categories vary. Do not treat all figures as directly interchangeable.

4. Current-year salary details:
The current Placement Records page lists selected “Highest Package” entries for 2023–24, 2024–25, and 2025–26. Average and median salaries for these years were not found in the visible text of that page.

5. Current placement rules:
A full, current placement policy/eligibility document was not located in the inspected pages. The report's “no active backlogs” definition and offer-category rule are documented, but their current applicability is not confirmed.

6. Internships:
The general internship page describes programme-dependent durations; the older report specifies six-month internships for named integrated M.Sc. programmes and MCA. The sources may reflect different levels of specificity or different periods.

7. Recruiter list:
The 2026 brochure calls its list “Few of our recruiters,” so it is illustrative rather than exhaustive. The 2021–2022 report has a department-wise dream-company list with formatting/OCR irregularities.

8. Contact listings:
The Placement Cell page and Placement Directory page list different staff roles/names and provide different details. Both are recorded above; no unsupported decision is made about which is more current.

9. Placement percentages, highest packages, and salaries:
No figures have been calculated from other figures in this file. All reported values retain their source labels and academic/placement years wherever those are provided.

10. Information not found in inspected official sources:
- A current complete placement policy with all eligibility rules and branch-wise criteria.
- Current average and median salary figures for 2023–24, 2024–25, and 2025–26.
- A single current consolidated table of number of students placed for every academic year.
- Complete current internship placement counts, company-wise internship numbers, and internship stipend amounts.
- A current full list of all recruiters and company-wise numbers of students hired.
- Specific entrepreneurship schemes, named partner banks, financial assistance amounts, application criteria, and current workshop schedules.
- A direct placement-office phone number explicitly labelled as such on the Placement Cell page.
